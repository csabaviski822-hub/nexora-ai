PAN 2: The AI That Can Imagine What Happens Next

Artificial intelligence is moving beyond predicting words and generating images. A new generation of systems is being designed to simulate how the physical world changes when an action takes place. PAN 2 is one example of this emerging technology.

What Is PAN 2?

On November 10, 2026, the Institute of Foundation Models (IFM) announced PAN 2, an open world model developed for physical intelligence. Instead of simply generating a response to a question, the system can take a starting scene and a written action, then generate a video showing a possible outcome.

This approach is based on a simple but powerful idea: an AI system may make better decisions if it can simulate the consequences of an action before carrying it out in the real world.

How Does a World Model Work?

A language model predicts what word is likely to come next. A world model attempts to predict how a scene or environment will change over time. For example, it may simulate what happens when an object falls, a door opens, or a hand moves an item across a table.

PAN 2 combines two major processes. Its conceptual reasoning component plans a sequence of events, while its perceptual component generates the visual details of the simulated scene.

Why Could This Matter for Robotics?

Robots often need to act in environments where mistakes can be expensive, dangerous or difficult to reverse. A system capable of simulating possible outcomes could help an AI agent compare different actions before choosing one.

  • Testing possible movements before a robot performs them.
  • Exploring how objects may react to physical contact.
  • Supporting planning in unfamiliar environments.
  • Helping researchers study physical cause and effect.

What Are the Limitations?

A generated simulation is not automatically a perfect representation of reality. Visual consistency, accurate physics and long-term predictions remain difficult challenges for world models.

PAN 2 is an important research development, but it should not be confused with a fully autonomous robot or a complete understanding of the physical world. Real-world testing and independent evaluation remain essential.

What Comes Next?

World models could become an important part of future AI systems, especially when combined with robotics, simulation and planning. The ability to test an action virtually before performing it physically could change how intelligent machines learn and operate.
